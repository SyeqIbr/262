How Constellate works

Every topic in the course is a star in your sky. You make stars brighter by doing the two things that work best for remembering: reading, then testing yourself.

How a star gets brighter

  1. Read the notes

    Short notes with diagrams and worked examples. Read one and your star starts Shining.

  2. Blurt it

    Close the notes and answer 20, 25 or 30 fresh questions from memory.

  3. Get it marked

    Green, amber or red for every answer. 80% or more in every subtopic and it's Blazing.

  4. Join the dots

    Light up the stars in a subject and they join into its constellation.

What is blurting?

Blurting means writing down everything you remember about a topic, without looking at your notes. It feels harder than re-reading, and that's the point: pulling knowledge out of your memory is what makes it stick. Psychologists call this retrieval practice.

On Constellate, you don't face a blank page. Claude writes 20, 25 or 30 fresh questions from the notes for that subtopic, so they always match the AQA spec. You answer from memory, then every answer is marked:

  • ✓ Correct The answer would get full credit.
  • ~ Nearly Right idea, but a key word is missing or it's too informal.
  • ✗ Not yet Wrong, or missing the point. You get the model answer.

Your score is simply how many you got right, like 19 / 25. Then you can retry just your ambers and reds, or ask for a whole new set.

Your sky

Your sky shows every topic in the course. Stars brighten on their own as you read and blurt: Faint → Shining → Blazing. A star starts shining as soon as you read a note or try a blurt, and it blazes once every subtopic's best blurt is 80% or more. Topics without notes yet are dotted stars that are still forming.

Tap a star to see each of its subtopics, with a red, amber or green dot for your best blurt. Your next star always tells you the single most useful thing to do next.

Already know a topic from class? Tap its star and choose Set this star myself. You'll be asked to confirm first, and your star gets a dashed ring so you can tell it apart. When your real progress catches up, it takes over.
