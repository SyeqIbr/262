Cell biology

Cells are the basic unit of all living things. This topic covers what cells are made of, how they divide, and how substances move in and out.

Cell structure

Plant and animal cells are eukaryotic: their genetic material is enclosed in a nucleus. Bacterial cells are prokaryotic: they are much smaller and have no nucleus.

Microscopy

Electron microscopes have a much higher magnification and resolution than light microscopes, so they show smaller structures such as ribosomes in detail. This has helped scientists understand what cells are made of.

magnification = image size ÷ real size

Both sizes must be in the same unit before you divide.

Converting units

mm × 1000 → µm × 1000 → nm

Example: an image of a cell is 5 mm wide. The real cell is 0.05 mm wide. Magnification = 5 ÷ 0.05 = × 100.

Exam tip: Magnification has no units, but always write it with a × sign, e.g. × 400.

Specialised cells

Cells become specialised through differentiation: they develop the parts they need for a particular job. Most animal cells differentiate early in life, but many plant cells can differentiate throughout their lives.

Animal cells

  • Sperm cell: tail to swim, lots of mitochondria for energy, and enzymes in the head to get into the egg.
  • Nerve cell: long, with branched ends to carry electrical impulses and connect to other cells.
  • Muscle cell: contains protein fibres that contract, plus many mitochondria.

Plant cells

  • Root hair cell: large surface area to absorb water and mineral ions.
  • Xylem: hollow tubes, strengthened by lignin, that carry water and minerals.
  • Phloem: carries dissolved sugars; the cells are joined end to end with sieve plates.

Cell division

The nucleus contains chromosomes, made of DNA. In body cells chromosomes come in pairs. Body cells divide in a series of stages called the cell cycle:

  • Growth: the cell grows, makes more ribosomes and mitochondria, and copies its DNA.
  • Mitosis: one set of chromosomes is pulled to each end of the cell, and the nucleus divides.
  • Division: the cytoplasm and cell membrane divide, making two identical cells.

Mitosis is needed for growth, repair and asexual reproduction.

Stem cells

A stem cell is an undifferentiated cell that can become other types of cell.

  • Embryonic stem cells can become almost any type of cell.
  • Adult stem cells (e.g. in bone marrow) can form many types of cell, including blood cells.
  • Meristems in plants (at root and shoot tips) can form any plant cell, throughout the plant's life.

Stem cells may help treat conditions such as diabetes and paralysis, but there are risks, such as transferring viral infections, and ethical objections to using embryos.

What speeds up diffusion?

  • A bigger difference in concentration (a steeper concentration gradient)
  • A higher temperature
  • A larger surface area of membrane

Active transport lets root hair cells take in mineral ions from very dilute soil water, and lets the gut absorb sugar into the blood.

Exam tip: For osmosis, always say "water" and "partially permeable membrane". Leaving either out can cost a mark.

Required practicals

  • Microscopy: use a light microscope to observe, draw and label plant and animal cells, including a scale or magnification.
  • Osmosis: investigate how different salt or sugar concentrations change the mass of plant tissue, such as potato cylinders.
  • Microbiology Triple only: investigate how antiseptics or antibiotics affect bacterial growth, by measuring the clear zones around discs on an agar plate.
