B1 Cell biology · Note 4 of 6

Mitosis & stem cells

In this note you'll learn to

  • Describe chromosomes and where they're found
  • Describe the three stages of the cell cycle
  • Name the types of stem cell and what they can become
  • Evaluate the benefits and risks of using stem cells

Chromosomes

The nucleus of a cell contains chromosomes made of DNA. Each chromosome carries a large number of genes. In body cells, chromosomes are normally found in pairs.

The cell cycle

Body cells divide in a series of stages called the cell cycle:

  1. Growth and DNA copyingThe cell grows and makes more ribosomes and mitochondria. It copies its DNA, so each chromosome now has two copies.
  2. MitosisOne set of chromosomes is pulled to each end of the cell, and the nucleus divides.
  3. DivisionThe cytoplasm and cell membrane divide, forming two cells that are genetically identical to the parent cell.

Cell division by mitosis is needed for growth, repair and asexual reproduction.

Exam tip: Use the exact phrase "genetically identical". Just saying "the same" can lose the mark.

Stem cells

A stem cell is an undifferentiated cell. It can divide to make more cells like itself, and it can differentiate into other types of cell.

TypeFound inCan become
Embryonic stem cellsHuman embryosAlmost any type of cell
Adult stem cellse.g. bone marrowMany types of cell, including blood cells
MeristemTips of plant roots and shootsAny type of plant cell, throughout the plant's life

Uses

  • Treating conditions such as diabetes and paralysis by replacing faulty cells.
  • Therapeutic cloning: an embryo is made with the same genes as the patient, so its stem cells aren't rejected by the patient's body.
  • Cloning plants from meristem tissue, quickly and cheaply, e.g. to save rare species from extinction or to grow large numbers of crops with useful features such as disease resistance.

Benefits and risks

BenefitsRisks and issues
Could treat diseases with no current cureViral infections could be passed on with the stem cells
Therapeutic cloning avoids rejectionSome people have ethical or religious objections to using embryos

Exam tip: "Evaluate" questions want both sides, then a conclusion that says which side you think is stronger and why.

Key words

Chromosome
A long strand of DNA, found in the nucleus, carrying many genes.
Gene
A small section of DNA on a chromosome.
Mitosis
The stage of the cell cycle when the nucleus divides.
Stem cell
An undifferentiated cell that can become other types of cell.
Meristem
Plant tissue containing stem cells, at root and shoot tips.
Therapeutic cloning
Making an embryo with the patient's genes, to give stem cells that won't be rejected.
