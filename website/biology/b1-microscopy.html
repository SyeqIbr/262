Microscopy

Electron microscopes have a much higher magnification and resolution than light microscopes. Resolution is the ability to tell two points apart, so electron microscopes show small structures such as ribosomes in detail.

Light microscopeElectron microscope
MagnificationLowerMuch higher
ResolutionLowerMuch higher
Living specimens?YesNo
Cost and sizeCheap and portableExpensive and large

Calculating magnification

magnification = image size ÷ real size

Both sizes must be in the same unit before you divide.

mm × 1000 → µm × 1000 → nm

Example: an image of a cell is 5 mm wide. The real cell is 0.05 mm wide. Magnification = 5 ÷ 0.05 = × 100.

Exam tip: Magnification has no units, but always write it with a × sign, e.g. × 400. You may also need standard form, e.g. 0.000 002 m = 2 × 10−6 m.

Required practical: microscopy

  • Place a thin layer of tissue (e.g. onion epidermis) on a slide and add a stain such as iodine.
  • Lower a coverslip on at an angle to avoid air bubbles.
  • Start with the lowest-power objective lens. Focus with the coarse knob, then the fine knob.
  • Draw what you see with a sharp pencil, using clear lines and no shading. Label the parts and add a magnification or scale.
