B1 Cell biology · Note 7 of 9

Diffusion

Diffusion is the spreading out of particles, resulting in a net movement from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration. It doesn't need energy.

Examples: oxygen and carbon dioxide moving in and out of cells during gas exchange, and the waste product urea moving from cells into the blood plasma, to be removed by the kidneys.

What speeds up diffusion?

  • A bigger difference in concentration (a steeper concentration gradient)
  • A higher temperature
  • A larger surface area of membrane

Surface area to volume ratio

Single-celled organisms have a large surface area compared with their volume, so diffusion across their surface is enough. Bigger organisms have a smaller surface area to volume ratio, so they need exchange surfaces and transport systems.

1 cm cube: surface area 6 cm², volume 1 cm³ → 6 : 1

A 2 cm cube: surface area 24 cm², volume 8 cm³ → 3 : 1. Bigger objects have a smaller ratio.

A good exchange surface has…

  • a large surface area
  • a thin membrane, so particles only travel a short distance
  • an efficient blood supply (in animals)
  • ventilation, for gas exchange (in animals)
