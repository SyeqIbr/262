Diffusion & exchange surfaces

In this note you'll learn to

  • Define diffusion and give examples
  • Explain the factors that affect the rate of diffusion
  • Calculate surface area to volume ratios
  • Explain how exchange surfaces are adapted

What is diffusion?

Diffusion is the spreading out of the particles of a gas or a solution, resulting in a net movement from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration. It happens because particles are always moving randomly. It doesn't need energy from the cell.

Examples of diffusion in living things:

  • Oxygen and carbon dioxide moving in and out of cells during gas exchange
  • The waste product urea moving from cells into the blood plasma, to be removed by the kidneys

What speeds up diffusion?

FactorEffect
Concentration gradientA bigger difference in concentration → faster diffusion
TemperatureHigher temperature → particles move faster → faster diffusion
Surface area of the membraneLarger surface area → more room for particles to cross → faster diffusion

Surface area to volume ratio

A single-celled organism has a large surface area compared with its volume, so diffusion across its surface is enough to meet its needs. Bigger organisms have a smaller surface area to volume ratio, so they need exchange surfaces and transport systems.

Worked example

Find the surface area to volume ratio of a 2 cm cube.

Surface area = 6 faces × (2 × 2) = 24 cm²

Volume = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 cm³

Ratio = 24 : 8 = 3 : 1

A 1 cm cube has a ratio of 6 : 1, so the bigger cube has the smaller ratio.

Exchange surfaces

A good exchange surface has:

  • a large surface area
  • a thin membrane, so particles only diffuse a short distance
  • an efficient blood supply (in animals), to keep the concentration gradient steep
  • ventilation, for gas exchange (in animals)
WhereAdaptation
Small intestineMillions of villi give a huge surface area; good blood supply
LungsMillions of alveoli with thin walls; good blood supply; ventilated by breathing
Fish gillsThin filaments with a large surface area and many blood vessels
Plant rootsRoot hair cells give a large surface area
LeavesFlat and thin, with stomata that let gases in and out

Key words

Diffusion
The net movement of particles from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration.
Concentration gradient
The difference in concentration between two areas.
Surface area to volume ratio
How much surface an object has compared with its size.
Exchange surface
A surface adapted for substances to pass through it, e.g. the alveoli.
