B1 Cell biology · Note 2 of 9

Specialised cells

Cells become specialised through differentiation: they develop the parts they need for a particular job. Most animal cells differentiate early in life. Many plant cells keep the ability to differentiate throughout their lives.

In mature animals, cell division is mainly used for repair and replacement.

Animal cells

  • Sperm cell: a tail to swim, lots of mitochondria for energy, and enzymes in the head to get into the egg.
  • Nerve cell: long, with branched ends to carry electrical impulses and connect to other cells.
  • Muscle cell: contains protein fibres that contract, plus many mitochondria.

Plant cells

  • Root hair cell: a large surface area to absorb water and mineral ions.
  • Xylem: hollow tubes, strengthened by lignin, that carry water and mineral ions.
  • Phloem: carries dissolved sugars; the cells are joined end to end with sieve plates.

Exam tip: Always link the feature to the job, e.g. "many mitochondria to release energy for swimming".
