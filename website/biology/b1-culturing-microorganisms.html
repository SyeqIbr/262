Culturing microorganisms

Triple only: this note is for GCSE Biology (8461), not Combined Science.

How bacteria multiply

Bacteria multiply by binary fission: one cell splits into two. In ideal conditions this can happen as often as once every 20 minutes, so numbers grow very quickly.

Bacteria can be grown in a nutrient broth (a liquid) or as colonies on an agar gel plate. To investigate them properly, the culture must not be contaminated by other microorganisms.

Aseptic technique

StepWhy
Sterilise Petri dishes and culture media before useKills any microorganisms already there
Pass the inoculating loop through a flameSterilises the loop
Tape the lid on, but don't seal it all the way roundLets oxygen in, so harmful anaerobic bacteria don't grow
Store the plate upside downStops condensation dripping onto the agar
Incubate at no more than 25 °C in school labsReduces the chance of growing harmful pathogens

Calculations

bacteria at the end = bacteria at the start × 2number of divisions

Worked example 1

A bacterium divides every 20 minutes. How many bacteria come from one cell after 2 hours?

2 hours = 120 minutes. 120 ÷ 20 = 6 divisions

1 × 26 = 64

64 bacteria

area of a clear zone = πr²

Worked example 2

A clear zone has a diameter of 12 mm. What is its area?

Radius = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 mm. Area = π × 6² = π × 36

Area = 113 mm² (to 3 significant figures)

Common mistake: using the diameter in πr². Halve it first to get the radius.

Required practical: microbiology

Aim: investigate how antiseptics or antibiotics affect bacterial growth.

  1. Spread the bacteriaUse a sterile spreader to cover an agar plate evenly with bacteria.
  2. Add the discsPlace paper discs soaked in different antiseptics or antibiotics on the plate, plus a control disc soaked in sterile water.
  3. IncubateTape the lid, turn the plate upside down, and leave it at 25 °C for about 48 hours.
  4. MeasureMeasure the diameter of the clear zone around each disc and work out its area.

A bigger clear zone means the substance is better at killing the bacteria or stopping them growing.

Key words

Binary fission
The way bacteria reproduce: one cell splits into two.
Aseptic technique
Methods that stop unwanted microorganisms contaminating a culture.
Agar
A jelly that bacteria are grown on, containing the nutrients they need.
Clear zone
The area around a disc where bacteria haven't grown.
