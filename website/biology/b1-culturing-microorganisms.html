Culturing microorganisms

Triple only: this note is for GCSE Biology (8461), not Combined Science.

Bacteria multiply by binary fission: one cell splits into two. In good conditions this can happen as often as once every 20 minutes.

Bacteria are grown in a nutrient broth or on an agar gel plate. To study them properly, the culture must not be contaminated by other microorganisms.

Aseptic technique

  • Sterilise Petri dishes and culture media before use, to kill unwanted microorganisms.
  • Pass inoculating loops through a flame to sterilise them.
  • Secure the lid with tape, but don't seal it all the way round, so oxygen can get in and harmful anaerobic bacteria don't grow.
  • Store the plate upside down so condensation doesn't drip onto the agar.
  • In school labs, incubate at no more than 25 °C to reduce the chance of growing harmful pathogens.

Calculations

bacteria at the end = bacteria at the start × 2number of divisions

Example: a cell divides every 20 minutes for 2 hours. That's 6 divisions, so 1 cell becomes 26 = 64 cells.

area of a clear zone = πr²

Used to compare how well antibiotics or antiseptics work.

Required practical: microbiology

Spread bacteria on an agar plate and add paper discs soaked in different antiseptics or antibiotics. After incubating, measure the clear zone around each disc. A bigger clear zone means the substance is better at killing the bacteria or stopping them growing.
